## Indirect lighting

- **The research scenes as seen in the client.** Three causes: the emitters' caustic photons carry light through glass that doesn't focus (speckles in lamp, modern-hall, spaceship); the surface cache's finest entries show as blocks and teeth (classroom, veach-ajar, cornell-box, the bathroom's mirror); the indirect light is slow to settle, with splotches moving meanwhile (kitchen, living-room, veach-bidir). docs/research-scenes-review-plan.md.
- **The thorough voxel test for every ray.** The hits' voxel choice is thorough (`GiHitVoxel<kThorough>`: other surfaces' voxels tested too, not only those marked apart) only for the main pass's near field. Try it for the reflections' near field (`GiNearTrace<false>`), the surface cache's and the probes' rays (`GiHitLitAt<false>`, `GiHitRadiance<false>`), each and together, and keep what clearly improves the picture for a modest cost. docs/notes/indirect-lighting.md, "One rule for every lookup and hit".
- **A voxel's surface chosen by coverage.** Deferred. The voxelizer keeps one surface a voxel, chosen by the order of its normal's bits, so at a coarse level something smaller than a voxel can take a voxel that is mostly another surface, and a wall meeting others in a corner can lose its voxels there (a dark line in bathroom2's niche). Choosing by coverage would cost the voxelizing on every scroll and every frame for what moves; keeping the frontmost surface instead brought up other artifacts. Revisit if such a corner shows. docs/notes/indirect-lighting.md, "The niche behind the statuettes".
- **bathroom2's light where it isn't.** A line along the ceiling's edge, from the space above it seen through a slot and lit by the last level; and young cache entries in the niche under the window show the probes' leaking light for a few dozen frames. Also: what testing where a voxel's light was gathered costs. docs/notes/indirect-lighting.md, "A voxel's light is taken only where it was gathered".
- **The clip levels' disagreement.** The levels disagree about the same place, which shows while walking as darker patches that travel with the camera (Sponza's corridor floor). The world grid steadies the walk but costs too much to be on by default (docs/world-cache-plan.md); next, a fade between the levels in the clipmap. docs/notes/indirect-lighting.md, "Why the clip levels disagree".
- **The surface cache's remaining noise.** MovingInterior's far wall between seeds; the leading strip of a fast turn (more samples for new pixels); Bistro at night's lit window interior varying from run to run. docs/notes/indirect-lighting.md.
- **`SPEEDO_GI_CACHE_FAST_SAMPLES=2` blows the light up** for seconds, where other values are alike: most likely the lead of a fast mean of 2 samples bouncing on. Either the lead needs a floor on the fast count, or the variable's range (0 to 1024) does.
- **The world grid's first seconds and its walk.** Settled, the grid is close to the clipmap, but a cell is filtered far less than a cache entry, so its first seconds are blotchier and a walk's moving frames are no nearer the settled ones. The cache's edge test in the cells' filter leaks (Sponza's arcade takes the courtyard's sky): it needs the cache filter's other tests first (what is between two cells, the planes), and passes that don't read each other's earlier results. docs/world-cache-plan.md.

## Glass and caustics

- **The emitters' caustics beyond the views.** Photons land only in the cells of what the views hold. What is out of every view takes the voxels' light straight through glass (a coarse level of cells for every landing would fix it), and so do the indirect lighting's voxels (the cells could hold their light, bouncing the caustics). Highlights through solid glass are the direct light's, since photons carry irradiance alone (a direction kept with it would let the specular use them). cornell-caustic's ceiling is darker than its reference's. docs/notes/rendering.md, "The emitters' caustics".
- **Refraction hits don't stamp the cache's blocks.** Reflection hits stamp the blocks they read (`GiMarkSeenHit`); the transmissive fragments' rays don't, since the stamps made that shader much slower by its size. A pass of its own for those stamps, or the next item, would allow it.
- **The traced hits' light sample out of the transmissive fragment shader.** A refraction's hit hidden from the screen takes a light sample (`TracedEmitterHidden`), and the rule telling glass that gathers light from glass that doesn't made the shader slower by its size alone. Direct light kept in cells of the world, as the caustics are, sampled in a compute pass for the cells the hits ask for, would take both out of it. docs/notes/rendering.md, "The emitters' caustics".
- **glass-of-water at 60 Hz.** The one research scene over the frame budget, most of it Main Transmissive: its glass, water, ice and bubbles refract through up to `SHADER_TYPES_CAUSTIC_SURFACE_COUNT` surfaces each. docs/research-scenes-plan.md, "Where it stands".
- **Rough glass closer to its references.** Step 6's lobe ray improved it, but lamp's frosted bulb still shows a thin dark rim, and past roughness 0.15 most of a rough surface's light is the transmission texture's mips: a blur by roughness alone, not by how far behind the surface the scene is. Next: the nearest glass layer's reflection and refraction each a lobe ray a pixel, filtered and accumulated by Reflection passes of their own, as the visibility buffer's glossy reflections are (without the filter it went wrong). A still view already averages up to 128 frames' rays (`GlassLight`); while the view moves it is one frame's ray, so a history reprojected by what is behind the glass would be the first part. Judge by lamp's bulb, bathroom2's jar and TransmissionRoughnessTest. docs/research-scenes-plan.md, C2 and step 6; docs/notes/rendering.md, "Traced reflections and refractions".

## Assets

- **Import time of large scenes.** Opening a research scene in a new user folder took minutes (a model and each of its textures reimported, then the BVH and ray query structures built), far too long for iterating. First measure where the time goes per phase (glTF parse, texture decode and compression, cache write, BVH build, structure build) with timestamps in the log, then optimize the largest: parallel texture imports on the executor, faster encoders, or caching what is rebuilt each open. docs/notes/assets.md, "Caches".

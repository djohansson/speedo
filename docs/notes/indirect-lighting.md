# Indirect lighting

This note describes the indirect lighting (global illumination): diffuse ambient light from world space irradiance probes (as in DDGI) whose rays trace a voxel clipmap or the scene's triangles, a surface cache that measures the light arriving at each surface voxel, a near field of short rays for contacts, and emissive surfaces sampled as area lights. Light is world anchored and lags a change by a blend. The code is in `src/gfx` (`gfx/indirectlight.h`, `gfx/bvh.h`, `gfx/emitters.h`, `gfx/accelerationstructures.h`) and `src/gfx/shaders` (`gi.slang`, `ginear.slang`, `giworld.slang`, `emitters.slang`, `bvh.slang`, `bvhtrace.slang`; shared structs such as `GiData` are in `capi.h`). It is on by default (View > Rendering Features > Global Illumination...; `SPEEDO_GI=0` starts without it). The sections go from the general to the particular: the design and the frame's flow, with the structures in the order a frame uses them (the clipmap and its voxels, the probes and their resolve, the surface cache, the near field, the emitters, the world cache); then tracing (triangles, hardware ray queries); then the rules that reconcile the clip levels, from the general ones to the corner cases; then the debug views and the neural cache experiment.

## Indirect lighting: irradiance probes over a voxel clipmap

Per frame: voxelize the static geometry (only when something changed), light the voxels, trace rays from the probes, update the probes, resolve their light into the voxels, update and filter the surface cache, then look the result up per pixel in the main pass. The passes, in order: GI Seen, GI Shift and GI Voxelize (when a clip moved or the scene changed), GI Keep, GI Light Static, GI Restore and GI Voxelize Moving (when something moves), GI Light, the flags, GI Trace, GI Probe Update, GI Resolve, the cache's passes (and the world cache's, GI World Clear and GI World Fold, where it is chosen), GI Sides, and the near field's (GI Near, GI Near X, GI Near Y, GI Near Temporal). The pre-pass is recorded before them since GI Seen reads its depth.

The Global Illumination window (View > Rendering Features > Global Illumination...) holds the switches and options: Emissive Lights, then under Indirect Lighting its checkbox and its options, greyed out while it is off: Rays (triangles or voxels), Resolution, Surface Cache, Light Store, Warm-Up Rays, Near Field, Near Field Contact, Near Field Rays and Near Field Over Frames. Light Store and Warm-Up Rays are greyed out without the surface cache, the near field's options without the near field (which runs only where rays trace triangles). Each option names its `SPEEDO_*` start value in its tooltip, listed in docs/environment-variables.md.

### The clipmap

- `SPEEDO_GI_CLIP_COUNT` levels (3 by default, at most `SHADER_TYPES_GI_LEVEL_COUNT`, 4), finest first (`gi::MakeClipmap`). The last covers the scene's bounds (`SPEEDO_GI_RESOLUTION`, 128 voxels along the longest axis by default; the Resolution menu offers `gi::kResolutions`, 64 to 384, and voxelizes the scene again) with a margin of one probe spacing. Each finer level is a cube of about the same voxel count (a multiple of 4 voxels a side), voxels half the size of the next, centered ahead of the eye on a grid of its probes and kept inside the last.
- The resolve's output is five `R16G16B16A16Sfloat` 3D images (`gGiVolumes`) holding the probes' ambient cube per voxel (`GiSample`), the clips' slabs stacked along z, in columns along x where `DeviceLimits::maxImageDimension3d` would be passed. They are storage images in the general layout. A new clipmap's buffers are cleared first (GI Clear); everything is made again when the model, bounds or draws change.
- In split screen each view has its own clips of every level but the last, which is shared, ordered by level so the clips containing a point, finest first, have lower indices. A point in two views' clips of a level takes both by weight. What starts from a coarser clip takes its view's (`GiData::coarser`).

### Moving clips

A clip moves by whole snaps (`gi::kSnapProbes`: one probe cell, 8 voxels), so what lights a point stays fixed in the world. Probes are laid out from the clip's corner (`GiData::probeOrigin`) and keep their state across a snap (`GiData::scroll`). Each clip has a box around its center it always holds well inside; its weight in the shading (`GiClipWeight`) is that box's alone, faded over 8 voxels, so a snap changes no point's weight (only where a clip is clamped to the last clip's edges is there an edge fade). Rays leave a clip at the whole box (`GiFadeBoxExit`); the shading takes each clip by its weight of what finer clips leave (`GiSampleAt`).

GI Shift (`ComputeGiShift`) gives each voxel the static one kept at its place before, or empty where it scrolled in, and only that is voxelized; a move of the clip's size or more, or a scene change, voxelizes all. Everything kept per voxel index must move with a scroll or be read at its place before it: voxels, kept light, visibility bits, cache page tables, seen stamps and `gGiAhead`.

### Voxelizing

- GI Voxelize runs a thread per triangle of the main list's static draws (`gGiJobs`). It samples the surface at under half a voxel apart into the voxel half a voxel behind each face (`GiVoxel`: albedo and occupancy, a normal facing away from the face, emission) with `InterlockedMax`. A triangle over 32 voxels long is rasterized along its dominant axis instead (`GiVoxelizeRasterize`). Albedo is base color times 1 minus metallic.
- Static voxels are kept (GI Keep) and lit (GI Light Static). While something moves (skinned, morphed or animated nodes) they are copied back (GI Restore) and overwritten by GI Voxelize Moving (placed by `WorldPosition`); what moves occludes, is lit and bounces light. With a paused animation (`FrameInputs::modelStill`) no clip is voxelized and light settles.
- Both faces always write (`GiVoxelFaces`, `kGiVoxelDoubleSided`; a drawn side's normal wins, `GiPackVoxelNormal`). A voxel only a single sided triangle's front face wrote holds a never drawn side and gets no light. For voxel walking rays a voxel only a back face wrote is passed unless its twin behind is hit within two voxels. What moves is not lit by the surface cache (`GiMovingSurface` is looked up as without it).

### The coarser clips in turn

The finest level's clips update every frame, coarser levels one level a frame (`GiData::updated`, `Renderer::InternalPlanGi`; `SPEEDO_GI_STAGGER=0` for all). All update in a frame that resets, starts the cache or dumps, while something moves, and for a while after a cache starts over. A clip not updated keeps all it holds. Each clip has a clock (`Renderer::GiClipClock`) advancing only on its updates (ray phase, probe halves, cache window), and what changed while it waited is taken at its next update (`relight`). A probe or entry new to a finer clip starts from the coarser clip's light (`GiCoarserHalf`).

### Lighting the voxels

- `GiDirectRadiance`: a voxel's emission plus albedo over pi times the punctual lights of its cell (`gi::BinLights`, a 16^3 grid) and the emissive surfaces where they are lights, from its front only, each shadowed by a ray through the voxels (`GiOccluderDistance`). Shadow rays start a voxel out along the normal.
- Each frame GI Light (`ComputeGiLight`) gives every voxel that light plus what its albedo reflects of the probes' light as last resolved (`GiData::bounce`, `SPEEDO_GI_BOUNCE=0` for none). The bounce starts a few frames after a reset, since probe light is provisional until then.
- The static voxels' light is kept (`ComputeGiLightStatic`) and made again when a clip is voxelized or the lights change (`Renderer::myGiLights`). The part the lights don't change (the emitters' light and how far each voxel is open, `GiOpenAlong`, a ray through the static BVH that bounds where the cache's rays start) is kept apart (`gGiKept`). A gradual change of the lights relights a share of voxels per update (`SPEEDO_GI_RELIGHT_PHASE_COUNT`).

### The flags

`gGiFlags` (`ComputeGiFlags`) holds per block of 4x4x4 voxels the frame stamp where the lighting found something. Bit 0 says something is in the 3x3x3 blocks around, bit 3 whether within 4 blocks along each axis. The resolve skips blocks without bit 0 and probes without bit 3, so a clip's cost follows its surfaces, not its volume. The third word is the last frame a lookup read the block; bit 4 of the first says the cache works on it.

### The probes

- **Layout.** A lattice every `SPEEDO_GI_PROBE_SPACING` voxels (8) from the clip's corner, so every point of a voxel interpolates the same 8; only probes near a surface trace. Two buffer halves swap each frame. A probe new to a clip takes the coarser clip's light as one frame's worth; `GiData::reset` starts all over.
- **State.** An ambient cube (`GiIrradianceBasis`: six faces of irradiance over pi along +-x, +-y, +-z, `gGiIrradiance`; `GiEvaluate` blends faces by the direction's squared components), chosen over spherical harmonics, which leaked a grazing bright source along the normal. Also an offset and, only where voxels are walked, a 16x16 octahedral depth map (`gGiProbeDepth`).
- **Rays.** `SPEEDO_GI_RAY_COUNT` (128) at most, reduced by `SPEEDO_GI_RAY_BUDGET` (500000) over the probes traced last time. Directions are a Fibonacci sphere turned by the frame's phase (`GiRayDirection`, `SPEEDO_GI_RAY_PHASE_COUNT` phases, 32), identical for every probe, each probe at its own phase (`GiProbePhase`) so ripples cancel in a lookup.
- **Update** (GI Probe Update). The irradiance is blended toward the mean of the probe's last whole cycle of ray phases (`GiData::cycleOffset`), not each frame's rays, so a small bright source does not make a cycle long sawtooth, with weight `SPEEDO_GI_HYSTERESIS` (0.97 at 60 frames a second, scaled by frame length where time based, `SPEEDO_GI_TIME_BASED=0` for per frame). `SPEEDO_GI_ADAPT=1` is adaptive hysteresis (off by default). Where the scene is still, a settled probe holds its state every second lap of phases instead of tracing (`SPEEDO_GI_PROBE_FREEZE=0` never).
- **Voxel walk** (`GiMarch`): each crossed voxel once, empty blocks crossed in one step, on through coarser clips and to the environment past the last. A step cap ends a walk and is counted and printed (`GI_STATISTIC_CAPPED_RAYS`), since a runaway walk hung a gpu; the loop has no `continue` and its capped-ray writes are only where it skips, for the drivers' sake.
- **Relocation** (`GiProbeOffset`). A probe moves out of voxels at its corner and, while settling, away from the nearest back face when over a quarter of its rays met backs. A probe still inside is marked (`kGiInsideShare`) and the resolve skips it.

### The resolve and the shading

- **Resolve.** GI Resolve (`ComputeGiResolve`, a group per 4x4x4 block with its 8 probes in shared memory) gives each voxel the faces of the 8 probes around its center, each weighed as DDGI does: by how much it faces the voxel's open side, and by whether it sees the voxel's point (with voxel walking, the probe's depth map; with triangles see "Tracing the triangles"), crushed below a fifth, floored at a thousandth, weighted trilinearly. A probe marked inside is skipped. A surface voxel is resolved as the voxel out along its open side.
- **Shading.** `Shade` takes `GiSampleSurface` in place of the environment's irradiance where a clip covers the point: the finest clip holding it more than a probe spacing from its edges, blended into the next over 8 voxels. The 8 voxels around the point offset along the normal and toward the eye (`GiSurfaceBias`) are read in each clip's own voxels, filtered, premultiplied faces over weights. The specular uses the same lookup: the environment by the indirect share of its irradiance, and by roughness the faces along the reflection.

### The surface cache

View > Rendering Features > Global Illumination... > Surface Cache, on by default (`SPEEDO_GI_CACHE=0`; `gGiCache`, `GiData::cache*`). The probes spread light through gaps narrower than a probe cell into cell wide spots; the cache measures the light per surface voxel at the voxel's size.

- **Entry.** Three uint4: six faces' rgb as half floats, sample and ray counts, the mean of the samples' squares, the open side (octahedral) and the rays that started inside something, plus words for the emitters' light.
- **Paging.** Only blocks that hold entries and were read lately have pages of 64 entries from a pool, found through a per clip page table (`gGiCacheBlocks`, two halves swapped each frame). A block keeps its page while near a surface, holding an entry and read within `SPEEDO_GI_CACHE_KEEP_FRAMES` (1024); `ComputeGiCacheRelease` frees the others and the pool grows on demand (GI Cache Grow).
- **What is traced and filtered.** Only what is looked up: `ComputeMain` and GI Seen stamp the blocks each pixel's lookup reads (`GiMarkSeen`), reflections' hits too; the resolve marks blocks active where they or a neighbour were read lately. Others trace a ray every 8th frame and take their own light unfiltered (`SPEEDO_GI_CACHE_EVERYWHERE=1` does all).
- **Update** (`GiCacheUpdate`, in the resolve). The entry gets `SPEEDO_GI_CACHE_RAYS` (1) rays from the voxel, mostly over the open side's hemisphere, into a running mean of at most `SPEEDO_GI_CACHE_SAMPLES` (4096; 32 where something moves) and at most half the rays since the cache started over. A young voxel traces more rays (the window's Warm-Up Rays, `SPEEDO_GI_CACHE_WARM_RAYS`: 4 by default, 4, 8 or 16 in the menu, 64 at most); a settled one where nothing moves traces every other frame. A voxel with over a quarter of its rays inside something gives no light. Lights changing or a new buffer start the cache over.
- **Filter** (`ComputeGiCacheFilter`, as SVGF). A noise pass, then a-trous passes (`SPEEDO_GI_CACHE_FILTER_PASSES`, 3 at most, their count by a reach in the world) weighing taps by the open sides' agreement, distance off the voxel's plane, confidence and light difference against `SPEEDO_GI_CACHE_SIGMA` deviations of the noise. Taps are left out when something crosses the way (`GiCacheFilterBlocked`) or when behind the voxel's surface. The last pass moves the volumes' light toward the filtered light.
- **Fast mean.** An entry also keeps the mean of its last `SPEEDO_GI_CACHE_FAST_SAMPLES` (16) samples and writes how far it is ahead of the whole mean (`gGiAhead`). The cache's rays add it to the bounced part of what a hit reflects (`GiHitLitAt`), so bounces do not wait on a whole window while the shown light is the whole mean.

### The near field

View > Rendering Features > Global Illumination... > Near Field, on by default (`SPEEDO_GI_NEAR=0`; `ginear.slang`, `GiData::near*`; where rays trace the BVHs). Where a solid rests on a surface, the contact's voxels are the solid's never drawn inside and the gap is thinner than a voxel, so volume lookups find no light there. Screen space rays cover it.

- **Rays.** Every `SPEEDO_GI_NEAR_STRIDE`-th pixel each way (4; Near Field Rays offers 2 or 4) of the pre-pass's depth traces a ray through the BVHs (GI Near) up to `SPEEDO_GI_NEAR_REACH` (8) voxels, in one of 16 cosine distributed directions over a 4x4 tile, turned every frame. The light is what the hit reflects with the lights' light taken at the hit by a shadow ray (`GiHitLitAt`), or the volume's light from the ray's end where it meets nothing (`GiNearTrace`).
- **Filter and frames.** GI Near X and Y filter at the traced pixels. GI Near Temporal runs at every pixel: it upsamples and blends into a history reprojected by the depth, in the images the frame's parity says (Near Field Over Frames, on by default, `SPEEDO_GI_NEAR_TEMPORAL=0` without; `SPEEDO_GI_NEAR_HISTORY_FRAME_COUNT` frames, 16, and `SPEEDO_GI_NEAR_STILL_FRAME_COUNT`, 256, while the view, model and lights are still).
- **Use.** Main's opaque fragments take the rays' light by how many met another surface nearer than a contact (Near Field Contact, `SPEEDO_GI_NEAR_CONTACT`, 2.5 voxels) and by how far the volume lookup is trusted (`GiLookupTrust`), so the rays' light alone stands in where the lookup sees no probe. `SPEEDO_GI_NEAR_SHOW` shows its parts.

### Emissive surfaces as lights

View > Rendering Features > Global Illumination... > Emissive Lights, on by default (`SPEEDO_EMISSIVE_LIGHTS=0`): emissive triangles light the scene as area lights, at the first hit and in the voxels, with soft shadows traced through the BVHs (`emitters.slang`, `gfx/emitters.h`). Indirect rays then take only the light what they meet reflects (`GiHitLitAt`; `GiMarch` takes a voxel's emission out).

- **The emitters** (`emitters::Build`, at upload): non moving triangles whose material emits, per instance in world space, and a light BVH over them (`gEmitterTree`: pbrt's BVHLightSampler). `EmitterPick` walks it choosing each child by its share of `EmitterImportance`.
- **Per traced pixel** (every `SPEEDO_EMITTER_STRIDE`-th pixel each way, 4). Emitter Sample resamples `SPEEDO_EMITTER_CANDIDATE_COUNT` (8) candidates (RIS) and Emitter Shade traces the chosen point's shadow ray and writes the irradiance over pi. Temporal and spatial reuse (`SPEEDO_EMITTER_REUSE`, `SPEEDO_EMITTER_SPATIAL_COUNT`) exist but are off by default. Below `SPEEDO_EMITTER_ROULETTE` of the frame's white the ray is traced by chance and weighed up.
- **Filter and frames.** Emitter X and Y filter at the traced pixels (`SPEEDO_EMITTER_FILTER_REACH`). Emitter Temporal runs at every pixel: it upsamples (`EmitterUpsample`) and blends into a history reprojected with jitter removed and kept only where the last surface matched, longer while the view is still (`SPEEDO_EMITTER_HISTORY_FRAME_COUNT`, `SPEEDO_EMITTER_STILL_FRAME_COUNT`). Accumulating per pixel avoids blocks under temporal upscaling's jitter. Main's opaque fragments add the base color times `EmitterIrradiance` to their direct light.
- **The voxels** (`EmitterVoxelIrradiance` in `GiDirectRadiance`): `SPEEDO_EMITTER_VOXEL_SAMPLE_COUNT` (4) points, shadowed like the punctual lights, in the static voxels' kept light and the moving ones' each frame. With the anchored cache, a static voxel's kept emitter light is its entry's.

### The world cache

View > Rendering Features > Global Illumination... > Light Store chooses where the surfaces' lookups take the surface cache's light from: Clipmap (the default), each level its own light, or World Grid (`SPEEDO_GI_WORLD=1`; `Scene::Settings::giWorldGrid`; `giworld.slang`), experimental. The grid is a hash table of cells anchored in the world at several sizes (`SPEEDO_GI_WORLD_LEVELS`, 5, each level twice the side of the one below; `SPEEDO_GI_WORLD_CELLS`, about a million, the least recently used dropped when full), filled by the surface cache's rays, so that the levels are means of the same samples and agree by construction. The design, steps and what it must replace are in docs/world-cache-plan.md.

- **A cell** is a level, the world position over that level's side and a normal bucket, so the two sides of a thin wall are different cells (`GiWorldKey`, `GiWorldHash`). Each sample of a valid cache entry is added to its cell at every level from its clip's up (`GiWorldAdd`), summed per frame by atomics and folded into each cell's running mean in GI World Fold (GI World Clear first). New cells start from their parent's light (`GiWorldSeed`). The fold's pass filters along the surface (`GiWorldFilter`).
- **A lookup** (`GiWorldFaces`, `GiWorldBlend` in `GiSampleSurface`) takes the level of the clipmap's voxel size at the point (`GiWorldClipLevel`), blended with the next by the fraction, trilinear across the surface over cells of its bucket, each cell by how much of the way to its samples is clear of the voxels (`GiWorldReach`), so light does not come through walls. Where the cells hold too little, or on what moves, the clipmap's light is read.
- **Side by side.** The clipmap stays built and running whatever the switch says: its rays fill the grid and it lights what moves, the air, what a traced ray meets and what the grid has no cell for yet. The grid's storage and passes exist only while it is chosen, and only the main pass reads it. The `gi-world` debug view shows it. Greyed out without the surface cache.

## Tracing

### Tracing the triangles

The default (View > Rendering Features > Global Illumination... > Rays; `Scene::Settings::traceTriangles`; `SPEEDO_GI_TRACE=voxels` for voxels).

- **BVH.** A model gets a BVH of what the voxels hold of it (`gfx::bvh::Build`, `gfx/bvh.h`): static triangle lists that are not blended or transmissive, per instance in world space. It is tinybvh's CWBVH (`ports/tinybvh` overlay) with spatial splits (`SPEEDO_BVH_BUILD=fast` for binned SAH), built on the executor at import and kept in the asset cache (`bvh::Version` is in the cache key: bump it when `Build` changes). It is uploaded with the model whichever rays are chosen, so switching is immediate.
- **Traversal** is in `bvh.slang`, tinybvh's kernel ported to Slang. A hit's normal, side and flags are read after traversal (`BvhFinish`, `BvhHit`). A backface is a single sided triangle seen from behind. Alpha masked triangles are cut out like the main pass's alpha test (`GiAlphaMask`). Every BVH triangle carries its material.
- **Uses.** The probes' rays (`GiTraceBvh`: one closest hit gives radiance and depth), shadow rays, references, the cache's rays, the near field and the emitters. At a hit the radiance is the voxels' (`GiHitLitAt`: the shell voxel half a voxel off the surface on the ray's side), so what changes against voxel walking is what a ray meets: the triangles, with no shells.
- **What moves** has its own binary layout BVH (`bvh::Moving`), placed per frame on the cpu in `Model::Animate` and refit, traced after the static one (`GiTraceTriangles`). The static voxels' kept light is not shadowed for good by what moves.
- **Resolve with triangles.** There are no probe depth maps. Whether each of a voxel's 8 probes sees it is a ray between them through the static BVH (a back face met means the probe is behind a wall and is skipped, a front face hides it), kept per voxel (`gGiVisibility`) and retraced only where a probe moved or is new, by a kernel of its own (`ComputeGiResolveTraced`). A probe weighs its facing's 4th power, hidden ones a thousandth of that and bringing the least light of theirs per face and color, so a pocket no probe sees into is shadowed.

### Hardware ray queries

- **Both variants.** Every shader compiles and works with `HARDWARE_RAY_QUERY` 1 and 0. The definition picks only how a ray is traced, the device's ray queries or gfx's BVHs, never whether a feature exists: no code is compiled out by it. Software tracing is the default on macOS and the only path on devices without ray tracing hardware, so a feature behind the hardware path would be missing there, and the two paths could not be compared image to image. Trace through `bvhtrace.slang`'s interface (`BvhTraceScene`, `BvhTraceGlass`, `BvhTraceSceneOrGlass`, `BvhTraceInstances`), which has both paths, and keep `#if HARDWARE_RAY_QUERY` to that file, the ray queries' bindings and the two wrappers with a path of their own (`GiTraceTriangles`, `ShadowOccluder`). What the BVHs' path costs more is a budget or default that goes by the device (`Scene::Settings`, the Ray Tracing panel), not a guard. The quick tier runs `scripts/test-assets/norayqueries.txt`'s models without ray queries, and the visual checks' `ray-queries` check compiles the other variant.
- **Selection.** Shaders compile with `HARDWARE_RAY_QUERY` where the device has ray queries (`DeviceLimits::rayQuery`, `WantsRayQueries` in rhi; part of the shader cache key); `SPEEDO_RAY_QUERY=0` keeps the BVHs. View > Rendering Features > Ray Tracing... > Ray Queries chooses Hardware or Software at run time (`Scene::Settings::hardwareRayQueries`); on macOS Software is the default and `SPEEDO_RAY_QUERY=1` opts in. A switch replaces the layouts that differ (`Pipeline::ReplaceLayout`) and reloads the model with that variant's structures.
- **Structures** (`gfx::AccelerationStructures`). A top level structure per frame index of three instances: what does not move (built once from the model's own indices and vertices, then compacted), what moves (refit each frame from corners placed by `Model::Animate`, `RecordUpdate`), and transmissive surfaces, which only glass paths query. Instance masks (`BVH_TRACE_MASK_*`) separate them. Alpha masked geometries are non opaque and their candidates go through `GiAlphaMask`. No CWBVH is built in this mode.
- **macOS.** KosmicKrisp has ray queries behind `MESA_KK_EXPERIMENTAL=ray_query` (set by the client when ray queries are asked for). Metal's ray tracing triggers two Apple bugs that restart the gpu ("CDM Kill timeout"): pipelines made and dispatched at once through an `MTL4CommandQueue` (`rtrepro compile`), and any compute kernel compiled with Metal ray tracing running beside other gpu work (`rtrepro frame`). The mesa port's workaround (`KK_WORKAROUND_63`) makes the restart rare, not impossible; recheck each macOS update and drop it when it no longer restarts. Never run several ray query clients at once on KosmicKrisp (`assettest.ps1` runs them one at a time) and do not run such experiments without asking.

## The levels disagree, and the rules that reconcile them

### Why the clip levels disagree

Each level lights its own voxels with its own rays, so a place's light depends on which level the camera's distance gives it. The difference lies in the probes' and cache entries' gathering (shade through one level at a time with `SPEEDO_GI_LOOKUP_LEVEL`), and has two causes, both of voxel size: a level measures light off the surface by its own voxels, so a band of sky light along a thin curtain is wider the coarser the level; and a coarse voxel at a thin sheet holds both sides. The finer level is the truer, so the levels must measure the same thing: the light arriving at the surface itself, per side, averaged over the voxel's footprint. The sections below are the rules that do this. Light kept once per place at several resolutions (the world cache, docs/world-cache-plan.md) removes the rest.

### The cache anchored at the surface

Where rays trace triangles, an entry is the light arriving at the surface itself (`GiCacheAnchor`): a ray from the voxel's open side toward a point of its footprint finds the triangle, and the entry's rays leave just off it, so a coarse entry is the mean of the finer ones in it. Voxels both faces of a triangle wrote hold entries where their normal is a drawn side's. Air voxels beside a surface take its light in the filter's last pass. A voxel new to its clip starts from nothing, not seeded from the coarser clip. In the last clip the probes' light stands in by its samples. In a finer clip the voxel holds its own light alone and how far its entry is from settled (`kGiYoungMark`, in the volumes' marks channel), and the lookups take that much less of the clip and leave it to the coarser ones (`GiSampleAt`): a clip that reaches a place comes in as its entries get their samples, from what the coarser clip showed there. The masks follow the same shares (`GiSidesClips`), and a lookup with masks reads no clip they are not about where a finer one lit the point (`GiSidesHas`). An anchor that finds a surface facing another way behind the voxel's own plane is past the edge of the voxel's surface and gives no sample. The older path, light measured at the voxel, remains only where rays walk voxels.

### The cache's rays' hits take the lights' light at the hit

The lights' part of a hit voxel's radiance is replaced by the voxel's albedo times the lights' irradiance at the hit (`GiHitLitAt`): one light of the hit's cell chosen by its unshadowed contribution, with a shadow ray from the hit. The rest stays the voxel's (bounce, emitters, emission), as do a moving voxel and a single sided triangle's back. Otherwise a coarse voxel half in sun lit rays that met its shaded side.

A voxel keeps the most albedo of what wrote it, so what a hit reflects is scaled by the hit triangle's own albedo over the voxel's, at most 1 (`GiHitShare`, the material's color and textures at the hit as the voxelizer takes them). Otherwise a dark surface sharing a voxel with a light one reflects as the light one, and light builds up between surfaces closer than a voxel.

### The emitters' light the voxels keep is their cache entries'

A static voxel keeps what arrives from the emitters and the direction it arrives from (`GiPackKept`), which a hit takes by its own normal. The entry takes an emitter sample every few of its rays, keeps their mean and writes it as the voxel's kept light (`EmitterLightSamples`); it scrolls with the voxel.

### The probes, resolved where they show

With the cache anchored, a settled entry's voxel shows the cache's light and almost none of the probes'. Probes are kept because the fog reads them (`AerialPerspectiveAmbient`), what moves is theirs, and the air's light is theirs (the near field's rays meeting nothing, light let through from behind, lookups beside voxels with few samples). Their cost is the resolve, not the rays, so their light stays in every voxel it was in and is resolved into a voxel only when that can change what it shows (`GiResolve`, `SPEEDO_GI_PROBES`, `SPEEDO_GI_PROBE_AIR_INTERVAL`): never into a voxel showing a settled entry's light, every frame into one with few samples, and into the rest every 8th update a block at a time (at once where a probe moved). Every voxel resolves after a reset, in a clip that scrolled, beside something that moves, while lights change, and where the cache is off. Where rays trace triangles the probes' depth maps are not made (`ComputeGiProbeUpdate`).

### A thin thing's two sides, apart

A voxel holds one side's light but a lookup reads 8 voxels, so where a board is thinner than a voxel they hold both sides. The voxelizer keeps which way the sides that wrote a voxel face; the cache's filter marks a voxel where the other side's entry holds another light (`GiFarSides`, `SPEEDO_GI_CACHE_THIN`), and a lookup reading a marked voxel loads its 8 and leaves out those whose surface its point is behind.

### What is behind a single sided surface stays behind it

Light from behind a single sided surface must not reach the room. A hit refuses a voxel whose entry is inside something (`GiHitVoxel`, `kGiRadianceInside`) or that only never drawn sides wrote with another normal, then looks at the six voxels beside for the triangle's own. The filter drops taps behind a surface the voxel is in front of (`kGiFilterBehind`), and lookups leave out a voxel behind a single sided surface they are in front of (`GiFarSides`).

### A voxel's light is taken only where it was gathered

An anchored entry's light is that of its apex (half a voxel out of the voxel's center along its normal), which at junctions can lie across a thin wall from a lookup reading the voxel. So a voxel's light is taken only where its apex sees the point taking it, by a short any-hit ray (`GiApexSees`). The lookups test from where the entry's rays start instead, on the surface under the voxel (`GiClipSees`, by the plane `gGiAhead` keeps): the apex is out in the open and sees past what the light was gathered behind, such as the edge of a ledge. The hits' voxel choice and the filter's marks keep the apex, with which they agree with what the entries gathered. The filter marks a voxel apart (`kGiApartMark`) at a junction where its apex does not see a neighbour's surface. GI Sides (`ComputeGiSides`) runs before the main pass: at each pixel near a marked voxel it tests the voxels the lookup reads and keeps a mask of those it sees (`GiSidesMask`, with its clip's level and the parity of the first voxel's coordinates); the main pass's opaque fragments leave out the others (`GiSidesHidden`), and voxels holding a surface facing far off the pixel's are left out without a ray (`GiSidesMaskFacing`). This rule lives only in the pass: in the lookup itself the shaders were slower and compiled much longer. The pass's point (the pre-pass's depth and stored normal) is a hair off the fragment's, so across a voxel's face the two find different voxels: the lookup finds the mask's voxels from its own point and corrects them by the stored parity, and a voxel the mask does not cover is read only where it has a share worth reading (`kGiSidesStrayShare`).

The pass sorts the surface voxels a lookup reads by how they face from the pixel's normal (`GiSidesBlock`): across or against it (`kGiFacingCosine`), always left out; its own (within `kGiOwnCosine`); and beside it, the rest. An air voxel is the surface's where the voxel behind it, along the axis the normal is most along, is one of its own; otherwise it is beside too, since its light is spread from whatever surfaces are around it. Voxels beside are left out wherever the lookup reads one of its own: what stands on a surface (a casing, a moulding) is another surface with other light, and the surface would take it over a voxel's width. Where the lookup reads none of its own, the pass looks a voxel along the surface (the nearer side on each of the two axes the normal is least along) and, if those 8 hold one, the mask says so (`GiSidesStep`) and the lookup reads them instead. Where neither has one, the voxels beside stay, as all the lookup has. A lookup's weight is that of the voxels left over their shares (`GiSampleClipWithout`), so leaving voxels out does not lower its trust against the near field's light (`GiLookupTrust`).

The pass's cost is its sight rays, and a thread group pays for the pixel that traces most. So the rays are traced one a turn of a single loop, each pixel taking the voxel it needs next, and only those that can change its mask (`GiSidesMaskFacing`): its own voxels first, then the air in front of them where one sees the point (those beside are left out then whatever they see), a stepped block's own voxels only where it has any (the ones shared with the lookup's block already tested), and the rest of the lookup's block only where no block has one of its own. A clip that holds a point leaves nothing of it to the next clip unless its voxels are young (`GiYoung` takes the other marks out), so a point is one clip's lookup and one clip's mask except where clips meet (docs/backlog.md, "GI Sides' cost").

### One rule for every lookup and hit

The rule that a voxel's light is taken only where its apex sees the point applies identically to the main view, the reflections and the indirect rays. Every lookup takes what its point may take light from as a parameter (`GiSeen`: per clip, a mask as `GiSidesMask` makes it): read from the GI Sides pass for the main pass's opaque fragments (`GiSeenFragment`), made at the point for a traced ray's hit (`GiSeenAt`), none where nothing is to be tested (`GiSeenNone`). The hits' path is generic on how far it tests (`GiHitVoxel<kThorough>`): thorough for the main pass's near field and the cache's and probes' rays (`GiTraceBvh`), not for the reflections' near field. Without it a hit takes any drawn voxel of the three along its normal, and at a thin wall lit from behind, a hit beside what crosses the wall takes the back's light.

A hit never takes a voxel whose surface faces against the side of the triangle the ray met: that voxel holds the other side's light. Where the clip that holds the hit has none but a refused voxel, the coarser clips are asked in turn (`GiHitVoxelFrom`), and a hit no clip has a voxel for is unknown (`kGiHitUnknown`): its ray is no sample for a cache entry and tells the near field nothing, rather than counting as black. A hit looks its voxel up once, whatever it takes of it (`GiHitLitAt`, its `lit`), and a kernel holds one variant of the lookup: the reference view's rays (`GiPixelReference`), compiled into the forward fragment shaders, use the near field's plain test there, not the cache's thorough one. The lookup is most of a hit's code, and those kernels are as large as KosmicKrisp compiles (docs/backlog.md, "The forward fragment kernels' size"). Taking such voxels for want of another made the room's light depend on which clip held its surfaces: the last clip's gave light from behind thin walls, a finer clip's (more voxels written only by unseen sides, lit with no light) took light away. Known limit: where voxels are much larger than a room's details, voxel sized patches remain.

### The niche behind the statuettes

The same rules in a hard case: a box of double sided sheets standing into a bright space behind a wall. The mask is tested on voxels pushed off the surface; wide filter taps past a drawn side facing against are left out; an anchor takes a surface far from the voxel's normal as none (`kGiAnchorFacing`); a hit refuses a voxel far off its triangle whose surface the ray came from behind (`kGiFarSideMargin`). Known limitation: a voxel stands for one surface, so at the last level thin objects against a wall take voxels that are mostly wall.

## Diagnostics and experiments

### Debug views and diagnostics

View > Debug View > Indirect Lighting (`SPEEDO_GI_DEBUG=<n>`) shows the indirect diffuse, the resolved weight, voxel radiance and albedo, which clip lights a point, the probes' state, the world cache's light (`gi-world`) and references (`GiPixelReference`, seconds a frame: capture after a few frames). The group is greyed out while the indirect lighting is off. `SPEEDO_GI_DUMP=<x,y,z>` or `pixel:<x>,<y>` prints the probe, volume and cache entry values at a point at exit, and per clip the 27 voxels around it (what wrote each, its normal, its light along each face, its marks, its own radiance): what a lookup or a hit there reads. Edit > Copy Scene writes the view as reproducing `SPEEDO_*` variables; an artifact report is a pose first.

### A neural radiance cache

A neural radiance cache in plain compute was benchmarked (`tools/neuralbench.cpp`, `neuralbench.slang`): a multiresolution hash grid of learned features decoded by a small network, trained by Adam, with integer atomics for the grid's gradients. Its cost is no obstacle, but it learns too slowly: it takes tens of steps to become usable whatever the size, rates or samples. Cells of running means at several resolutions given the same samples converge in far fewer steps for a tenth of the cost, and their coarser levels are the same samples' means, so they agree. So the replacement for the levels' light, if one is made, is world anchored cells of means at several resolutions (the world cache, docs/world-cache-plan.md), not a network; the network stays an option for what cells cannot hold, such as a glossy lobe's radiance by direction. Slang's neural module (`slang/neural.slang`) is experimental and its accelerated layers are cooperative matrices (none on D3D12), so an opt in for such hardware would be per backend behind an rhi capability.
